# Other Stakeholders Perspective

## Stakeholder Profile: Counsellors

### Role
Mental health counsellors, social workers, and psychiatric professionals provide psychosocial support to donors, particularly for H2S positive cases where disclosure anxiety, depression, stigma, and social determinants complicate care.

### Primary Goals
1. **Enable confidential disclosure support** - Create safe space for donors to share mental health concerns, stigma fears
2. **Address social determinants** - Understand occupational risk, housing instability, substance use contributing to H2S risk
3. **Improve care adherence through emotional support** - Help donors overcome barriers to getting treatment

### Key Needs
- **Confidential counselling session tracking** - Document sessions, progress, referrals; separate from medical record
- **Trigger identification** - Alert when new H2S positive donor needs psychosocial assessment
- **Specialist referral capability** - Route severe cases (suicidal ideation, addiction) to psychiatric specialists
- **Donor consent & privacy controls** - Only counsellor can see session notes; strict access controls
- **Integration with care team** - Alert doctor/hospital if counselling identifies barriers to care (poverty, stigma, addiction)

### Success Metrics
- **Counselling completion rate ≥70%** of referred cases
- **Donor trust score ≥85%** on confidentiality/privacy
- **Care adherence improvement post-counselling** ≥20% (donors who get counselled are more likely to follow through)

### What Kills It
- **Confidentiality breach** - If counselling notes leak or merge with medical record, donors lose trust
- **Mandatory disclosure to other teams** - Donors won't share if everything goes to doctors
- **No time for counselling in workflow** - If referral to counsellor is optional, it gets skipped

## Stakeholder Profile: Labs & Diagnostic Services

### Role
Labs process blood samples, run TTI/hemoglobin/comorbidity screening, and provide results that drive clinical decisions and care referrals.

### Primary Goals
1. **Standardize result reporting** - Ensure results are clear, consistent, actionable across all blood banks
2. **Reduce turnaround time** - Get results to doctors quickly so care decisions aren't delayed
3. **Integrate seamlessly with blood bank workflows** - Results flow to hospitals/doctors automatically

### Key Needs
- **Results standardization** - Define standard result format, thresholds, interpretive comments
- **Automated result delivery** - Results push to blood bank, hospital, doctor; no manual routing
- **Quality control tracking** - Document all QC checks, reagent lots, validation for audit
- **Integration with blood bank IT** - Auto-sync with collection system, no manual data entry
- **Bulk result upload capability** - Handle 100+ samples daily without bottlenecks

### Success Metrics
- **Result turnaround ≤24 hours** for TTI/comorbidity screening
- **Result accuracy 99.5%** in spot-check validation
- **Reporting format compliance 100%** with blood bank and hospital standards
- **Zero lost/misdirected results** due to system integration

### What Kills It
- **Results get lost or delayed** - Delays care, damages relationships with blood banks
- **Format incompatibility** - Lab sends results; hospital can't parse them; manual rework required
- **Quality concerns** - If results are questioned, lab's credibility is damaged
- **Integration failures** - New system breaks existing lab-to-blood bank workflows

## Stakeholder Profile: Insurers & Health Insurance Companies

### Role
Insurance companies assess risk, pool donors, and potentially cover preventive care or treatment for H2S and related conditions to reduce long-term claims.

### Primary Goals
1. **Build actuarial models** - Understand H2S risk, donor health patterns, care outcomes at population level
2. **Enable group insurance** - Offer pooled insurance to donors, blood banks; reduce adverse selection
3. **Demonstrate prevention ROI** - Show that early care for H2S flags saves money long-term vs. emergency care

### Key Needs
- **Anonymous aggregate population data** - Age, gender, geography, H2S prevalence, comorbidities; no PII
- **Outcome data integration** - If insured donor gets care, track outcomes (improved health, reduced hospitalizations)
- **Risk stratification model** - Segment donors: low/medium/high H2S risk for pricing/coverage decisions
- **Claims linkage capability** - If covered, donor's claims data links back to H2S screening for outcomes evaluation
- **Privacy-compliant data sharing** - All data anonymized, aggregated, compliant with insurance regulations

### Success Metrics
- **Enrollment in group policies: ≥1000 donors** within 12 months
- **Actuarial risk reduction: 20%+** for covered H2S cases through prevention
- **Claims cost reduction: 15%+** for early-care cohort vs. control
- **Coverage expansion** - Eventually offer H2S screening + preventive care as bundled product

### What Kills It
- **Data breaches or privacy violations** - Insurance data is gold for identity theft; one breach ends the program
- **Poor outcome data** - Can't prove ROI if outcomes are incomplete or unreliable
- **Adverse selection** - Sick people over-enroll; healthy people don't; pool becomes unprofitable
- **Regulatory rejection** - Insurance regulators block product due to discrimination or privacy concerns

## Stakeholder Profile: NGOs & Civil Society Organizations

### Role
NGOs provide community outreach, donor advocacy, social support, and often coordinate reactive flagging campaigns when population-level risks are identified.

### Primary Goals
1. **Enable community awareness** - Reach donors proactively about H2S screening benefits, care availability
2. **Support reactive flagging** - When government identifies H2S outbreak/high-risk area, NGO mobilizes community
3. **Advocate for donor rights** - Ensure donors understand their rights, aren't coerced, have access to care

### Key Needs
- **Outreach coordination capability** - NGO receives list of high-risk areas/populations, coordinates outreach campaign
- **Donor education materials** - Simple, multilingual resources on H2S, screening, care (can be generated by platform)
- **Reactive alert mechanism** - Government identifies cluster; platform alerts NGO to mobilize support
- **Impact tracking** - "Our outreach led to X donors showing up for care, Y outcomes improved"
- **Community feedback loop** - NGO hears donor concerns, feeds back to platform for improvement

### Success Metrics
- **Outreach reach: ≥50% of high-risk communities** per campaign
- **Care engagement from outreach: ≥40%** of reached donors complete screening + get care
- **Community satisfaction: ≥75%** on trust, cultural sensitivity, understanding

### What Kills It
- **No coordination or delayed alerts** - NGO learns about outbreak from WhatsApp, not official channel
- **Insensitive or inaccurate materials** - Outreach materials offend or mislead; community backlash
- **No feedback on impact** - NGO spends effort, can't track if it led to actual care
- **Privacy concerns in community** - Donor list shared with NGO, community finds out, privacy panic
