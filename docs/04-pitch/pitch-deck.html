The problem

Blood supply depends on donors who come back. Three things push them away.

India collects 1.57 crore units a year. Repeat voluntary donors are the safest supply. Yet every donation ends in silence.

1 · The gift disappears

Up to three patients. Zero news.

A donation is split into red cells, platelets and plasma that reach different patients. The donor gets a certificate and silence.

Sweden texts donors when their blood is used, to bring them back. That text says only that it was used.

The Independent, 2015
2 · The health check goes nowhere

Measured every time. Explained never.

BP and haemoglobin are measured at every visit; the sample is screened. Donors who pass take nothing home.

Results alone don't help: Taiwan's donor screening found "limited benefits… without additional interventions".

Vox Sang 2025
3 · Turned away, then gone

"Come back later." They don't.

High BP or low haemoglobin means deferral, with no explanation and no path back. 57% of Indian women are anaemic.

Deferred first-time donors return at about half the rate of others.

NFHS-5; Custer 2007

TraceDrop · the donor at the centre

Three reasons to give again, in one app, with one assistant: Drop.

Impact

See every life your blood touches

  • Every part of every donation followed to the patient: the treatment and type of hospital.
  • The patient stays anonymous: details shown only when at least 5 patients share them.
  • A short Story when a part lands, ending on a shareable postcard.
  • Badges for milestones; Circles for family, workplace and friends.
Health

Stay well until you can give again

  • The donation's own results, in plain words.
  • A finding becomes a Goal: one target, 2–3 steps, each citing its guidance.
  • Check-ins every two weeks, check-up slots, reminders you ask for.
  • The date you can donate again. At most one new Card a day.
Cover

Better cover for staying healthy

  • Like a credit limit: cover steps ₹5 → 6 → 7 → 8 lakh at the same premium.
  • Lower risk from a better health score creates room for more cover.
  • Only with your consent; the insurer sees the score, never results.
  • Illustrative in the prototype.

Around the donor: what their blood meant for the blood centre, hospitals, labs and public health · a network-wide ecosystem view · guided booking with a Google Calendar event · Your data, which lists and deletes everything kept.

Who wins, and how it scales

The donor pays nothing. Everyone who needs donors to return has a reason to fund it.

Donors

  • See what their blood did, privately
  • Understand their own results
  • A clear path back after a deferral
  • Cover that rises as they stay well
  • Control: Your data, Choices, delete

Institutions

  • Blood centres, SBTCs: donors who return
  • Employers: a wellness benefit on their group policy
  • Insurers: lower-risk members, with consent
  • Public health: an anonymised BP, sugar and anaemia signal
  • Patients: a steadier blood supply

Path to scale

  • Company camps at a few Bengaluru centres
  • All donors at those centres
  • SBTCs and eRaktKosh
  • Other states and languages
  • JAPAC blood services

Built to be trusted

No diagnosis or prescribing · urgent findings always say "see a doctor today" · patients anonymous by construction · insurer sharing off unless chosen · no inducement: deferred donors get the same follow-up · synthetic data only · every simulated part labelled as such.

Open before a pilot: a legal view on cover and the no-inducement rule · a partner insurer's rating factors · a clinical advisor's sign-off7
